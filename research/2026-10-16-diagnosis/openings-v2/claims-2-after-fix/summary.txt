CLAIM CHECK OF THE APP'S TEACHING TEXT (run 2026-10-16)

What this is: a program read every opening comment and every tactics text in the app, picked out
sentences that state something about the board that a program can test ("the knight attacks the
queen", "the bishop is pinned", "you are a pawn up", "Rf1 is checkmate"), and tested each one on the
position the text belongs to. Rerun it any time with: npm run check:claims

How to read the verdicts:
  true             the board agrees with the sentence (under at least one reasonable reading of it)
  false            the board disagrees under every reasonable reading the program could find
  could-not-parse  the program was not sure what the sentence means, or which position it is about
                   (a plan, an "if", a question, a hedge); it never calls those false

Text checked: 3378 pieces of text, 5643 sentences.
Board claims read: 2261. True: 1914. False: 0. Read but not judged: 347.
Clauses with a claim word ("attacks", "safe", "check", ...) that matched no pattern: 742 (most are not board claims: "safety check", "your king is safe").
Stockfish: /opt/homebrew/bin/stockfish, 1 thread (so runs repeat exactly), depth 14 for material and safety look-ahead. Run time: 23 s.

Coverage: 1574 of 2410 sentences with a claim word got at least one claim judged true or false.
Limits: the program understands a small, fixed set of sentence patterns. Plans, ideas, comparisons
("better", "active", "weak") and stories it cannot replay move by move are not checked at all. A "true"
here is a board fact, not a judgement that the move or the advice is good (that is the engine audit's job).
